\title{When Better Prediction Does Not Reliably Improve Trading:
A Reproducible ANN--MPS Evaluation in FinRL}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Aarav Nagar}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Quantum-inspired tensor networks can provide compact predictive
representations, but lower prediction error need not improve a sequential
trading policy. We evaluate a classically simulated matrix product state (MPS)
return signal against an artificial neural network (ANN) signal and the
standard FinRL state under matched Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
conditions. The protocol separates representation validation from downstream
policy evaluation, prespecifies PPO-budget and MPS-capacity selection, and
retains negative or unstable results. A three-seed capacity pilot found all
tested MPS bond dimensions within one percent validation mean-squared error;
the frozen parsimony rule selected bond dimension two (97 parameters).
Pilot trading differences changed across seeds. In the corrected final
comparison, mean Sharpe was 0.821 for ANN and 0.784 for MPS; the paired
MPS-minus-ANN difference was -0.037 with a paired-seed bootstrap 95\% interval
of [-0.113, 0.045]. MPS was higher in three of ten seeds. In a prespecified
equal-length robustness panel, mean MPS-minus-ANN Sharpe was +0.086, +0.092,
and +0.140 for 2017--2018, 2019--2020, and 2021--2022. The latter two
seed-bootstrap intervals included zero. In the first cell,
MPS mean Sharpe was 0.714 versus 0.627 for ANN.
The five-year primary result had the opposite sign. The evidence indicates
horizon and training-window sensitivity rather than a stable MPS advantage.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
FinRL, matrix product state, tensor network, proximal policy optimization,

\section{Introduction}

Financial reinforcement learning couples noisy predictive information to a
policy that must act under costs, path dependence, and changing market
conditions. FinRL provides a layered framework for constructing and evaluating
such trading agents \cite{liu2020finrl}, while PPO offers a widely used
clipped-policy-gradient objective \cite{schulman2017ppo}. These ingredients make
downstream portfolio behavior, rather than prediction error alone, the
appropriate endpoint for a trading representation.

Tensor networks have been proposed as compact, quantum-inspired supervised
models \cite{stoudenmire2016supervised}. They are related to broader quantum
machine-learning research \cite{biamonte2017quantum}, but a classical tensor
network does not imply quantum execution or quantum advantage. The resulting
research question is: \emph{under matched PPO training conditions, does
appending a validation-selected, classically simulated MPS return signal
improve out-of-sample trading performance relative to an ANN signal or the
standard FinRL state?} The question concerns incremental decision value, not
whether an MPS can fit returns, and it makes no directional assumption about
an MPS benefit.

This study makes three contributions. First, it operationalizes a
quantum-inspired MPS as a frozen predictive state signal inside a FinRL
benchmark while keeping the PPO policy, market data, costs, and evaluation
period classical and matched. Second, it separates representation selection
from policy evaluation: PPO budget and MPS capacity follow prespecified rules,
and final ANN--MPS comparisons use paired policy seeds and uncertainty
summaries. Third, it provides a claim-to-artifact workflow that preserves
capacity sensitivity, seed variation, and null, negative, or unstable outcomes
instead of selecting whichever configuration favors MPS.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Financial deep reinforcement learning.}
PPO constrains policy updates with a clipped surrogate objective and reuses
sampled trajectories for multiple minibatch epochs
\cite{schulman2017ppo}. FinRL organizes market data, trading environments, DRL
agents, and backtesting into a reproducible application pipeline
\cite{liu2020finrl}; FinRL-Meta extends this direction with reusable data
engineering and market environments \cite{liu2021finrlmeta}. These frameworks
make controlled trading experiments practical, but they do not establish that
a predictor with lower supervised error supplies useful information to a
cost-aware policy. Moreover, deep-RL comparisons can vary materially across
random seeds and implementation choices \cite{henderson2018deep}. We therefore
treat the learned signal as the intervention, hold the FinRL/PPO pipeline fixed,
and report paired policy-seed outcomes rather than a single best run.

\textbf{Tensor networks as learned representations.}
MPS models factorize a high-order weight tensor into a chain of low-order
cores. Stoudenmire and Schwab showed that this structure can support
quantum-inspired supervised learning with parameter growth controlled by bond
dimension \cite{stoudenmire2016supervised}; later work demonstrated
automatic-gradient MPS classifiers on image benchmarks
\cite{efthymiou2019tensornetwork}. Those studies evaluate supervised
prediction, not sequential portfolio decisions. Here, the MPS is likewise a
classical contracted model, but it predicts next-day returns and is frozen
before its signal is appended to the trading state. Bond dimension is treated
as a capacity choice, and test-period prediction or trading outcomes cannot
select it.

\textbf{Quantum and tensor-network reinforcement learning.}
Quantum machine learning broadly includes both algorithms intended for quantum
hardware and classically executed models inspired by quantum representations
\cite{biamonte2017quantum}. More specifically, Liu and Fang reformulate a
finite Markov decision process as a variational energy-minimization problem and
use an MPS to approximate the policy, evaluating the method on Gridworld
\cite{liu2020quantumtensor}. Our study does not reproduce that algorithm: it
does not encode the PPO policy as an MPS, reformulate the Bellman equation as a
Hamiltonian, execute a quantum circuit, or claim quantum speedup. It tests a
narrower translational question: whether a classically trained MPS predictor,
used only as an additional state signal, changes downstream PPO trading under
matched historical-market conditions. This separation between predictive
representation and policy optimization is the central distinction from both
supervised tensor-network benchmarks and tensor-network policy learning.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data and temporal separation}

The experiment uses 15 Nasdaq stocks from a saved, checksum-verified processed
dataset. Encoder fitting uses 2013--2017 observations, 2018 is reserved for
encoder validation and early stopping, PPO policies train on 2013--2018, and
portfolio evaluation uses 2019--2023. Neither encoder nor PPO fitting uses the
2019--2023 return target. Encoder-fit and validation rows are retained only
when their next-trading-day target also remains inside the corresponding
period, preventing a boundary target from entering model selection. A recorded
FinRL commit and dependency lock bind the software environment.

\subsection{Representations and state construction}

Both encoders receive 13 per-stock inputs and predict next-day return. The ANN
uses fully connected layers. The MPS maps the same inputs to a classically
contracted tensor network; its bond dimension controls capacity. The base
FinRL state contains cash, prices, holdings, and ten technical indicators for
181 values. Signal conditions append one frozen prediction per stock, producing
a 196-value state. Thus ANN and MPS policy states differ only in the final 15
values.

\subsection{Matched PPO evaluation}

Every condition uses the same two-layer, 64-unit PPO policy/value network,
initial portfolio of \$1,000,000, maximum order of 100 shares, reward scaling
of \(10^{-4}\), and 0.10\% transaction cost on executed buys and sells. A
prespecified budget pilot selected 20,000 environment steps because outcomes
and training diagnostics still changed between 10,000 and 20,000 steps; this
selection does not establish convergence.

\subsection{Capacity selection and primary estimand}

Bond dimensions 2, 4, and 8 are evaluated at the selected PPO budget using
matched pilot seeds 0--2. The frozen rule finds the minimum 2018 validation
MSE, treats configurations within 1\% as practically tied, and chooses the
fewest parameters, with fit time used only as a final tie-breaker. Test-period
prediction and trading metrics cannot select the dimension.

The primary final estimand is the mean paired MPS-minus-ANN Sharpe difference
across PPO seeds 0--9. We report every seed, the standard deviation, a
deterministic paired-seed bootstrap interval, and an exact two-sided sign test.
These quantities describe training-seed uncertainty on one historical split;
they are not causal or population-level intervals. A secondary protocol,
frozen before its outcomes were inspected, shifts encoder fitting through
2015, encoder validation and PPO fitting through 2016, and portfolio evaluation
to 2017--2018. It retains the same models, 20,000-step budget, costs, and seeds.
A further protocol, also frozen before its new outcomes, compares three
non-overlapping two-year evaluations: 2017--2018, 2019--2020, and 2021--2022.
Each has a four-year PPO training span, with three encoder-fit years followed
by one validation year. The first cell reuses the completed shifted run; the
other two refit encoders and PPO chronologically. This panel controls
evaluation- and training-span length but cannot isolate a causal market-regime
effect.

\section{Capacity Pilot}

All three capacity configurations fell within 1\% of the minimum validation
MSE (Table~\ref{tab:capacity}). The frozen rule selected bond dimension 2
because its 97 parameters were fewer than the 369 and 1,441 parameters of
dimensions 4 and 8. The longer measured fit time for dimension 2 did not enter
the tie-break because parameter counts differed.

\section{Final Evaluation}

Table~\ref{tab:final} reports all-condition summaries across the ten matched
PPO seeds after the boundary-corrected rerun. ANN had the highest mean Sharpe
(0.821), followed by MPS (0.784) and Base FinRL (0.689). The mean paired
MPS-minus-ANN Sharpe difference was -0.037 (standard deviation 0.134; median
-0.087). MPS exceeded ANN in three seeds and trailed it in seven
(Fig.~\ref{fig:final-effect}); the exact two-sided sign-test \(p\)-value was
0.344.

prediction MSE than ANN (1.504 versus 1.515), yet its mean downstream Sharpe
was lower. This is the failure mode motivating the paper: a predictive
improvement need not survive policy optimization, transaction costs, and
path-dependent portfolio dynamics.

\subsection{Temporal robustness}

Table~\ref{tab:equal-windows} reports the fixed equal-length panel. Mean
MPS-minus-ANN Sharpe is positive in all three two-year cells. The
2017--2018 interval excludes zero narrowly, while the 2019--2020 and
2021--2022 intervals include zero and seed dispersion increases in the last
cell. Mean Sharpe was 0.714 for MPS, 0.627 for ANN in the reused first cell;
the paired MPS-minus-ANN mean was +0.086 with a paired-seed bootstrap interval
of [0.001, 0.195]. The original shifted-window annualized-return difference
remains +2.34 percentage points with a 20-day moving-block-bootstrap interval of
[-0.83, 5.98] percentage points.

MPS test MSE is slightly lower in the first two cells but higher in
2021--2022 (1.525 versus 1.506 for ANN), even though its mean trading Sharpe
is higher in all three. Thus prediction-quality differences do not explain
the portfolio pattern monotonically. Together with the negative five-year
primary estimate, the panel supports sensitivity to evaluation horizon and
training-window design, not a stable architecture-wide advantage.


\subsection{Exploratory market-state diagnostic}

After the equal-window outcomes were known, a separately declared post-hoc
audit partitioned each window using the saved equal-weight benchmark. On
benchmark-down days, MPS-minus-ANN annualized mean-return differences were
-6.63, -0.56, and -2.15 percentage points; on nonnegative days they were
+8.56, +4.99, and +9.94 points. Every direction-state seed-bootstrap interval
included zero. The pattern is therefore only a clue that the positive
two-year means arose through upside capture rather than downside protection;
it is not a causal market-regime result.
\section{Limitations}

The study uses one small 15-stock universe, one five-year primary evaluation,
and three two-year secondary windows. Two secondary windows fall inside the
primary calendar span, so these are not independent replications.
The MPS is reconstructed from the stated research direction rather than an
unavailable original implementation. Capacity matching is exact only for the
reference dimension-4 ANN comparison; the selected dimension-2 model is a
parsimony-controlled sensitivity choice. The market-state diagnostic is
post-hoc, its states overlap, and three windows cannot represent the population
of market regimes. Its intervals describe seed variation rather than
historical-sample uncertainty. PPO optimization can remain unstable at the
largest tested budget, and ten seeds still provide limited uncertainty
resolution. Backtests omit several production frictions, including market
impact, borrow constraints, and latency. Most importantly, all tensor
operations execute classically; no quantum circuit or quantum hardware is
used.

\section{Conclusion}

Representation validation, policy evaluation, and capacity selection must
remain distinct. The frozen rule selected a compact MPS without using trading
outcomes, but the corrected primary comparison did not establish an MPS
advantage: its slightly lower prediction error accompanied a mean paired
Sharpe difference of -0.037 with an interval spanning zero. All three
equal-length two-year cells have positive mean differences, but the two new
intervals also span zero and prediction-quality direction is inconsistent.
The opposite five-year and two-year patterns are bounded, classical MPS
evidence of evaluation-window sensitivity and training-window dependence.
The exploratory market-state split suggests upside capture rather than
downside protection, but every direction-state interval spans zero. These
results show no stable advantage and are not evidence against tensor networks
generally.
